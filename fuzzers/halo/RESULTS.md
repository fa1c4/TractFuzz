# Halo Magma results

The fixed 60-row applicability and pilot campaigns are recorded in `results/halo/status.csv` and the provenance audit `results/halo/applicability.csv`. Each row's `detail` points to its Magma canary campaign CSV; raw build, monitor, fuzzer, and inference files live under `magma/work/halo/<bug>/`. The audit records the observed campaign duration separately from end-to-end setup, build, and campaign time. `reach_count` and `trigger_count` are Magma canary outcomes; `halo_reached` is the directed target-map observation. They must not be conflated.

The runtime library, CLI and control runners, wrappers, and linker pass used for these pilots are archived with SHA-256 hashes under `results/halo/method-build/`. The audit uses the archived runner digests so later rebuilds do not change the recorded pilot provenance.

The 2026-10-27 pilot reached `campaign_complete` on all 60 fixed rows: 45 in-process entries and 15 command-line entries. Every completed row has a built binary, seed-corpus digest, Halo input trace, checkpoint, and at least one generated Halo input. Magma recorded reach events on 42 rows and trigger events on 16 rows; 15 trigger rows have a saved input replayed through Magma. PNG003's aggregate trigger count has no retained witness. These counts describe one engineering pilot per row, not detection probability or comparative efficacy.

`results/halo/campaign-budgets.json` records 54 standard 60-second campaigns and six longer seed-import retries: SSL001 (300 seconds), SSL020 and PDF009 (180 seconds), and PDF006, PDF011, PDF019 (240 seconds). The longer rows are included as runnable fixed entries but excluded from equal-budget comparisons with the 60-second rows. `results/halo/applicability.csv` records each row's exact budget and observed campaign duration.

The independent Halo approximation has no confirmed author-code comparison. Controlled repeated comparisons with AFLGo on the same seeds and execution budgets have not yet been run. Pilot outcomes should not be interpreted as paper replication statistics.

The first fixed-entry gates passed on 2026-10-27: PNG006 completed with 127,507 reported LibAFL executions, 185,167 Magma reach events and no trigger in its 60-second campaign; TIF006 completed through the command-line adapter with 1,740 instrumented child executions and no Magma reach or trigger. The two runners produced versioned Halo traces and checkpoints. PDF003's aggregate monitor recorded 27 trigger events, and its saved queue input was replayed through the Magma monitor as a trigger. PNG003 reported aggregate trigger events but has no retained trigger witness; its count is not a replay-verified discovery.

PDF020 was built and fuzzed on its fixed `pdf_fuzzer` entry. Its 60-second pilot reported 427 LibAFL executions, 35 generated inputs, zero Magma reach/trigger events, and no directed target call in the linked binary. This is an attempted campaign with an instrumentation/reachability limitation, not an excluded row or a proof of unreachability.

The fixed TIF006 CLI target also has a two-arm, same-binary 60-second ablation at `results/halo/comparison-TIF006.json`, produced by `scripts/halo_compare_cli.py`. Both arms used the same 21 seed files, target SHA-256, seed-corpus SHA-256, and RNG seed 17. Halo-only reported 1,345 executions and the directed-havoc control reported 1,722; neither reached or triggered TIF006 in this short run. The control uses LibAFLGo distance feedback and its CLI distance-power havoc stage with the same entry adapter. It does not include the original in-process AFLGo CmpLog/MOPT sequence, so this is a stage ablation rather than the full AFLGo comparison requested by the plan.

The same two-arm ablation on PDF003 used 120 seconds per arm and the same 392 seeds, target SHA-256, seed-corpus SHA-256, and RNG seed 17. Halo-only reported 1,589 executions, 796 Magma reach events, and 63 aggregate trigger events; the directed-havoc control reported 1,686 executions, 57 reach events, and 27 trigger events. `scripts/halo_replay_comparison.py` replayed a retained trigger input from each arm; both arms retained the same input. This is one exploratory trial with different generated-input sequences, not a statistical claim that Halo outperforms the control.
